# Electrology handbook for Evergreen

Parent synthesis of five specialist research assignments. Research snapshot: **November 17, 2026**. Scope: a studio in **American Canyon, California**, targeting **summer 2027**. Read alongside [project memory](PROJECT_MEMORY.md), the [corrections register](CORRECTIONS_AND_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md), and the cited reports. This handbook guides design and planning; it is not a finalized treatment protocol, consent instrument, legal opinion, or verification that the business is ready to open.

## 1. What the research means for this project

The proposed small studio does not need to become a large medical-practice platform. It does need a reliable way to distinguish an inquiry, a health-history submission, informed consent, practitioner review, an appointment, and a treatment record. Low volume makes a simple workflow sensible; it does not make a public form or an email inbox appropriate storage for the entire sensitive record.

The selected direction remains a custom private intake and consent system, with ordinary email for minimal notifications and Square for scheduling as authorized later. A paper alternative remains useful. No subscription, vendor, production database, clinical policy, or legal entity was created by this research.

Five conclusions should shape every later build:

1. **Use clients and accurate credentials.** California licenses electrology separately from medical practice. A health questionnaire is still relevant to responsible practice.
2. **Support practitioner judgment.** A checked box can prompt review; it cannot establish diagnosis, device compatibility, or treatment clearance.
3. **Keep evidence and history.** Preserve the actual signed wording, submitted answers, review, subsequent changes, and a usable client copy.
4. **Determine privacy obligations from activities and data flows.** Neither the word client nor the presence of health questions settles HIPAA or California-law applicability.
5. **Design for the actual 2027 business.** The premises, practitioner, equipment, insurer, entity, and offered services remain factual inputs, not details that software should invent.

## 2. Practice, outcomes, and terminology

California's scope covers hair removal/destruction with an electric needle, including thermolysis; the license does not itself authorize medicine or surgery. AEA describes electrology as an allied health profession. These statements concern different classification systems and are not contradictory. “Client,” “electrologist,” “consultation,” “treatment,” and “health history” suit Evergreen's ordinary language. Do not imply that the practitioner diagnoses endocrine disease, prescribes medicine, or holds a medical license. See [practice report §§1–3](research/01-practice-and-evidence.md) and [California report](research/03-california-local-law.md); primary authority is [BPC §§7316 and 7320](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/laws_regs/act_regs.pdf).

Galvanic uses direct current, thermolysis uses high-frequency alternating current, and blend combines their effects. The actual choice depends on practitioner assessment and the equipment. Public explanations can describe these mechanisms without recommending a modality to an individual or presenting a universal ranking.

Electrolysis can permanently remove unwanted hair, ordinarily through repeat appointments. Visible clearance after one visit is different from a completed course or a long-term outcome. Growth cycles, prior removal, individual response, and newly emerging hair affect expectations. Do not guarantee total hours, completion dates, one-pass success, painlessness, or zero risk. The reviewed primary studies include small historical comparisons; neither “there are no studies” nor a precise universal success calculator is justified. The [practice report §§4–7](research/01-practice-and-evidence.md) records populations, outcomes, access limitations, and source dates.

FDA regulation of a device does not mean FDA approval of the studio or its procedure. Needle-type epilators are Class I, with a qualified premarket-notification exemption. Verify the exact device before any device-specific claim; avoid “FDA-approved studio” and sweeping “only FDA-approved” language. [21 CFR §878.5350](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-878/subpart-F/section-878.5350).

## 3. Screening, consent, and continuing review

A focused questionnaire should collect information the practitioner can use, with a reason for each required field. Separate electronic implants from passive dental metal. Ask relevant questions about medications/products, allergies and prior reactions, skin condition, healing/bleeding concerns, recent procedures, prior hair removal, and treatment goals. Permit uncertainty and private discussion. A missing answer is not “no.” The [safety report](research/02-safety-intake-consent.md) contains the review matrix and sources, including manufacturer limitations.

Braces, dental implants, pregnancy, medication use, or a diagnosis must not become blanket software exclusions. Equally, absence of a flag cannot become a safety guarantee. The practitioner evaluates the actual area, modality, device instructions, and relevant healthcare information. A physician's letter cannot expand the electrologist's scope or override a device prohibition. California also expressly excludes HIV/AIDS and hepatitis B from the communicable-disease category in its applicable service restriction; broad disease-based automatic rejection would be a particularly poor design. [16 CCR §984](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/laws_regs/art12.shtml).

The proposed journey is:

**Minimal inquiry → private invitation → intake submitted → practitioner consultation/review → documented consent and decision → appointment/treatment → aftercare and updates.**

This is a conceptual sequence, not a requirement to delay every scheduling action until every document is complete. Consultation scheduling, treatment scheduling, signed status, and practitioner review need separate states. An unresolved concern stays visible until an authorized person records its resolution.

Consent is a discussion and continuing choice, supported by a record. Explain the proposed service, material risks and limitations, reasonable alternatives including deferral, questions, and the ability to pause or stop. Keep marketing texts and publication of photographs separate from treatment consent. Distinguish documentation photographs from marketing permission. Do not present a signature as waiving all professional responsibility. AEA's [2026 practice standards](https://professionals.electrology.com/be-an-electrologist/standards-of-practice.html) are professional guidance, not proof that a particular form satisfies every California requirement.

At return visits, record relevant changes and the response to previous treatment. Keep original records and dated amendments. The practitioner must approve final question wording, aftercare, referral pathways, and treatment-related policies using training and actual equipment instructions. This research deliberately supplies no machine settings, medication changes, numbing doses, or individualized treatment clearance.

## 4. Infection prevention and problems after treatment

The operational system needs clean-to-dirty separation, suitable hand hygiene and gloves, sterile probes, appropriate reusable-tool processing, environmental cleaning, protected storage, sharps handling, and enough turnaround time and instrument inventory. A wipe is not a substitute for sterilization of an item that requires it. Follow the complete current rules and exact manufacturer's instructions, not this summary alone. [California Article 12](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/laws_regs/art12.shtml); [safety report](research/02-safety-intake-consent.md).

The following schedules must stay distinct:

| Control | Source and status |
|---|---|
| Chemical indicator on each sterilized package | Current California §982 requirement |
| Weekly check that sterilization equipment reaches the required temperature | Current California §982 requirement |
| At least monthly biological monitoring of each sterilizer | AEA professional standard, January 2025 |
| At least weekly biological monitoring | CDC guidance for healthcare facilities |
| Monthly independent-laboratory testing in a California proposal | Proposed text, not the adopted rule verified in this review |

The older project research already made the main distinction correctly. A final studio procedure must reconcile current law, professional standards, equipment instructions, monitoring failures, quarantine/reprocessing, logs, and maintenance. Recheck the pending California rule before opening. See the [AEA infection standards](https://professionals.electrology.com/pdf/Infection-Prevention-Standards.pdf) and [CDC §16](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/disinfection-sterilization/summary-recommendations.html).

Aftercare needs approved instructions and a usable reporting route. Do not automatically reassure every client that a worsening reaction is normal, diagnose from a photograph, or recommend internet numbing practices. Unexpected reactions and sharps exposure need documented response and appropriate medical evaluation. Employee exposure duties depend on actual employment and tasks. The safety report covers escalation and [Cal/OSHA §5193](https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/5193.html), with employee records kept distinct from client retention.

## 5. California and American Canyon requirements

Licensing and location are separate dependencies: active individual licensure, establishment coverage, the actual operator, city business authorization and zoning, suitable premises/accessibility, commercial waste arrangements, and applicable employment duties. A website, GitHub organization, or Square account establishes none of these. Shared-suite licensing can use building-wide coverage or individual suite licenses under the Board's published guidance; verify the actual arrangement. Home operation has additional premises and city requirements. [California/local report and requirements matrix](research/03-california-local-law.md).

**SB 1363, Chapter 233 of 2026, expressly authorizes licensed partnerships and LLC operators, operative January 1, 2027.** This materially updates the older LLC uncertainty for the intended summer 2027 opening. It does not prove that Evergreen has formed an entity or qualified for a license. Verify the operative statute, current application, entity standing and real ownership details when acting. [Board November 16 materials, agenda 11(b) and 12, PDF pp.89 and 91](https://www.barbercosmo.ca.gov/about_us/meetings/materials/20261116_materials.pdf); [Governor signing announcement](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/14/governor-newsom-signs-legislation-9-14-2026/).

American Canyon is in Napa County. Welcoming Solano and Marin residents does not mean the studio has establishments in those counties. Napa's commercial waste program includes relevant beauty facilities; business sharps should not be routed into a household program by assumption. Determine requirements using the actual generator, premises and disposal method. [Napa Medical Waste Program](https://www.napacounty.gov/3977/Medical-Waste-Program).

This review did not establish a universal California electrologist client-record retention duration or a complete minor-consent rule. Those remain targeted questions, not exemptions. Set the minors policy and a defensible retention schedule using applicable law, insurer terms and actual services; preserve records when a complaint or claim requires it. Do not copy another state's rule or an employee-record period.

## 6. Privacy, signatures, and the custom application

HIPAA depends on covered-entity/business-associate definitions and actual activities, including specified electronic transactions. Email and health-history questions alone do not settle it. California confidentiality, reasonable-security, breach-notification and website-privacy obligations require separate analysis; a small business can fall outside CCPA thresholds while still having other duties. CMIA's application-related provisions need specific attention before the custom system stores real health information. [Privacy report and applicability matrix](research/04-privacy-digital-records.md); [HHS covered entities](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html).

The incident plan must use current deadlines: SB 446 changed California breach-notification timing effective in 2026. Subject to the statute's conditions and exceptions, consumer notice is generally due within 30 calendar days; when more than 500 California residents require notification, the Attorney General sample notice is due within 15 calendar days after notifying consumers. This is not a reason to wait for day 30 or to ignore smaller breaches. See [privacy report §1](research/04-privacy-digital-records.md) for section references and [official legislative confirmation](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2025-12/2025-legislative-bill-summary.pdf).

An electronic signature does not require purchasing a signature subscription as a general rule. Its usefulness depends on intent, attribution, agreement to electronic handling where applicable, the record presented, integrity and accessibility. The signed wording and answers must remain reproducible. A drawn squiggle alone proves little; a typed-name approach with a clear signing action and suitable evidence can be more accessible. Exact sufficiency depends on the document and circumstances. The privacy report separates UETA/ESIGN mechanics from informed consent and any special authorization requirements.

Proposed minimum architecture, subject to the final data-flow review:

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Public website/contact form | Collect minimal contact details; direct private questions into the protected process |
| Private invitation and accessible form | Use unguessable, scoped, expiring access; no health information in URLs or ordinary analytics |
| Server and private record store | Validate requests, enforce access per record, encrypt appropriately, preserve versions and signed evidence |
| Staff interface with strong authentication/MFA | Review submissions, unresolved matters, amendments and consent without sharing a login |
| Minimal notifications | Notify that a packet is available; keep health answers and consent PDFs out of ordinary notification emails |
| Export, backup and incident procedures | Produce faithful records, recover from failure, manage retention/holds, revoke access and respond to exposure |

An invitation link is a credential: handle forwarding, theft, expiry, reissue and subsequent access deliberately. An unpublished URL, `robots.txt`, or a hidden dashboard does not provide authorization. Avoid third-party advertising, session replay and unneeded scripts on sensitive pages. Keep real records out of Git and development fixtures.

There is no final hosting decision yet. Cloudflare's published BAA availability is Enterprise-only; ownership of the domain does not establish suitability of a free hosting plan for every data flow. Vendor agreements, feature restrictions and the applicable legal framework must match. Custom development trades subscription fees for maintenance, security and recovery responsibilities; it is not a guarantee of zero ongoing cost. [Privacy report](research/04-privacy-digital-records.md); [Cloudflare compliance information](https://www.cloudflare.com/trust-hub/us-privacy-compliance/).

## 7. Operating quality and honest local marketing

Common failure modes are mismatched expectations, rushed turnaround, missed health updates, unclear appointment duration or fees, unsuitable communications, and weak complaint follow-up. The reports do not establish reliable prevalence rates. Address these with clear explanations, realistic scheduling, practitioner-authored notes, neutral reminders, consistent policies and a small review routine. Do not infer treatment time from calendar length or silently compress cleaning and documentation when bookings increase. See the [operations report](research/05-operations-common-problems.md).

Respect names, pronouns, privacy, accessibility and individual goals without demanding unrelated disclosure. Sensitive or presurgical services require actual competence and the relevant instructions; they should not be assumed part of launch scope. Complaints need factual records and appropriate follow-up, not automated clinical advice or public disclosure of a client's treatment history.

Keep American Canyon central to location content. Other counties are places clients may travel from. A fixed studio is not a Google service-area business merely because clients come from multiple counties. Publish accurate identity, address, hours, verified credentials, useful treatment explanations and directions. Avoid duplicated location pages, fake locations, invented testimonials, review incentives, and promised rankings. Accessibility applies to both the experience and the digital workflow; use testable accessibility targets without misapplying government-only deadlines to a private studio. The operations report supplies official Google, FTC, DOJ and California sources.

Preserve the owner-confirmed November 15 prices in [project memory](PROJECT_MEMORY.md). Future fee policies need clear, lawful disclosure; do not add mandatory service fees after advertising a lower price. Cash and card decisions remain open. No marketing or policy change was implemented in this research.
