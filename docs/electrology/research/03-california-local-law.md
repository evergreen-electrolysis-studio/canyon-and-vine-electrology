# California and American Canyon electrology requirements

Research cut-off: **2026-11-17**. Intended opening: **summer 2027**. Researcher: agent 3 of five. This is a planning reference; an item marked unresolved is not a finding of exemption. Sources below are primary government sources except the explicitly identified American Electrology Association (AEA) professional standard. No agency was contacted and no application was submitted.

## Findings that change the plan

California licenses electrology through the Board of Barbering and Cosmetology. BPC 7316(g) defines its hair-removal scope; BPC 7320 expressly withholds authority to practice medicine or surgery. “Client” is appropriate business language, but terminology does not remove licensing, infection-control, waste, or privacy obligations. The custom intake should help the electrologist evaluate whether to proceed within scope, not diagnose a condition or independently authorize treatment. [S1, S2]

**The historical LLC warning needs a dated update.** The Board’s November 16, 2026 materials identify SB 1363 as chaptered September 14, 2026, Chapter 233, Statutes of 2026, and describe it as operative January 1, 2027. Its changes expressly authorize partnerships and limited liability companies to engage in the regulated services with valid Board licensing. The Governor’s signing notice independently confirms signing. This is enacted future-operative legislation, not merely a bill under consideration. [S3, S4]

For a summer 2027 opening, an LLC is therefore a viable ownership path to evaluate. This finding does not establish that Evergreen has formed an LLC or obtained any license. A GitHub organization, domain registration, logo, and bank/payment account are separate from legal formation and professional authorization. Preserve the historical July concern as history; replace a standing “LLCs cannot operate” assumption with verification of the actual applicant and the 2027 application requirements. The current Board application already includes a corporation/LLC option and requests an active Secretary of State registration, legal name, FEIN, and controlling-person information. [S5]

## Consent, minors, and retention: what is established and what is not

AEA’s current Standards of Practice recommend written informed consent before treatment, an opportunity for questions, and a parent/guardian signature for a minor. This is a professional standard, not proof that California has enacted the same wording or a universal age rule. The standard also treats consent as withdrawable, which is a useful design principle: a completed online packet should not prevent the client from declining or stopping the service. [S21]

The targeted search of the Board Act, regulations, FAQs and official search results did **not establish a California electrologist-specific minimum client age, mandatory minor-consent form, or universal client-record retention duration**. Negative search results are not evidence that no other rule applies. Do not copy a tattoo statute, another state’s electrology retention period, a therapist’s seven-year rule, or an OSHA employee-record period into the studio’s client policy.

Before accepting minors, settle an actual business policy with the treating electrologist and insurer, and obtain narrowly scoped California advice if authority/capacity or document wording remains unclear. The research recommendation is to make adult/minor status, guardian identity/authority, the client’s own agreement, treatment-area restrictions, and any presence requirement explicit. These are proposed safeguards; they are not presented as a discovered statewide electrolysis statute. If minors are not part of the launch service, a simple adult-only launch policy avoids building an unneeded guardian workflow now.

Record retention similarly needs an explicit decision. Ask the insurer for claims-related retention requirements and obtain advice on a defensible California schedule, including minors and preservation when a complaint or claim is pending. Do not announce “we delete all records on request” before the applicable obligations are resolved. Build configurable retention and preservation, exportable signed records, and restricted access; avoid an irreversible automatic purge while the schedule is undecided. Privacy-law applicability and electronic signature requirements belong in the companion privacy/consent report, not inferences from whether the business calls its customers clients.

The health questionnaire should use professional judgment rather than a universal deny list. A medical history question can be relevant to safe cosmetic service without changing the professional license. Its answer is not a diagnosis, and absence of a flagged answer is not a guarantee. Separate “needs electrologist review,” “defer pending clarification,” and documented treatment consent. Require the electrologist’s review before treatment rather than letting a software checkbox make the final suitability decision.

## Current law, future law, and professional standards

Use the operative version for the action date. The 2026 Board Act compilation remains useful for current practice, while the identified 2027 amendment is relevant to the planned opening. The November 16 meeting’s separate cleanup legislative proposal is a proposal; agenda discussion itself does not amend the Act. The Board’s revised disciplinary guidelines approved in 2026 are listed as effective July 1, 2026, so they are already operative at this research date. [S2–S4, S22]

For sterilization, preserve separate labels for current 16 CCR 982, manufacturer requirements, and any AEA/CDC standard the studio adopts. The companion safety research identifies the proposed monthly laboratory biological-testing amendment as on hold in August 2026; the parent independently confirmed continuing on-hold status in the November 16 materials, agenda 14, PDF page 108. Do not describe it as enacted California law, and do not relabel the current weekly temperature check as a weekly biological test. This distinction in the older business-plan work was already sound; the newly identified LLC transition is a separate update.

The service area is a marketing fact. An American Canyon studio may attract Napa, Solano and Marin residents without the studio physically operating in each county. If services later occur at another location or in clients’ homes, reassess the actual new location and authorized service model instead of reusing this studio-only analysis. Booking software or a website cannot create an exception to location rules.

## Smallest outstanding decisions before implementation

1. **Practitioner and operator:** supply the real license details, choose the legal owner, and establish whether any entity already exists. Verify rather than fabricate these details in the site and forms.
2. **Premises:** identify the address and arrangement: commercial lease, salon suite, or residence. Obtain property-specific city clearance and establish whose Board license covers the space. This is the main dependency for practical local requirements.
3. **Napa waste:** describe the sharps generated, estimated quantity, storage location and disposal provider to determine the County application and any shared-facility conditions. No broad legal opinion is needed to ask this factual question.
4. **Minors, consent and records:** decide whether minors are offered at launch; obtain insurer requirements and resolve the remaining California consent/retention questions before final forms and deletion rules are approved.
5. **2027 refresh:** recheck the operative Act, Board application, local requirements and relevant safety-rule status shortly before licensing and opening. Store the new source date with every changed rule.
