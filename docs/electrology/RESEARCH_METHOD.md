# Research method and maintenance

Research snapshot: **2026-11-17**. Target jurisdiction: California, with an American Canyon establishment in Napa County. Clients may travel from other counties; that alone does not relocate the establishment or its regulator.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Binding law:** effective statutes and adopted regulations, with jurisdiction and effective date.
2. **Regulator guidance:** California Board, city/county, FDA, HHS/CMS, FTC, Cal/OSHA, and other competent agencies. Guidance is not automatically a new legal mandate.
3. **Professional standards:** AEA or other relevant professional bodies. Attribute them and distinguish them from California law.
4. **Manufacturer instructions:** exact device/model and current instructions for use. Do not infer instructions from a different device or vendor marketing.
5. **Research evidence:** original studies where available; state limitations, population, modality, and confidence. Lack of a good study does not prove a procedure effective or ineffective.
6. **Operational inference:** our design or business recommendation, labeled as such.
7. **Anecdote/commercial claim:** useful for discovering recurring problems, not prevalence, legal authority, efficacy, or treatment clearance.

## Required claim metadata

For material claims record publisher/title, direct URL, access date, source date where available, relevant section/page, authority level, conclusion supported, and limitations. For laws, distinguish enactment from effectiveness; for proposed rules record that they are not adopted law. A search snippet or older local document is a lead, not sufficient verification for a high-stakes conclusion.

Keep quotations brief. Prefer accurate paraphrase and precise section references over copying source text. Do not use another state's form, retention rule, scope, or infection-control schedule as California law.

## Reconciliation

- Preserve conflicting claims in the corrections register until resolved.
- Give newer verified evidence its appropriate authority; a later vendor blog does not displace a statute.
- Never silently upgrade a recommendation to a requirement or downgrade a requirement to a preference.
- If current official sources conflict, state the conflict and identify a targeted verification route.
- Distinguish absent evidence from evidence of absence: an unfound retention rule does not establish that records may be deleted whenever desired.
- Treat marketing copy, vendor pricing, plan limits, and local permits as volatile.

## Review triggers

| Trigger | Required review |
|---|---|
| Website/SEO copy change | Terminology, claims, licenses, service area, user-approved prices |
| Intake/consent implementation | Practitioner-approved content, scope, privacy applicability, signature record, review workflow, accessibility |
| Storage/authentication/provider change | Vendor terms, required agreements, data flow, access, logs, recovery, deletion/export |
| New device, modality, service, medication policy, or adverse event | Practitioner/manufacturer guidance and relevant safety sources |
| Location, owner/entity, staff, or insurance change | Licensing, local use, contracts, waste, workplace and privacy implications |
| Before summer 2027 launch | Fresh check of adopted/effective law, licenses, actual premises, policies, and all real-data workflows |

This is a review rule, not a scheduled automation. No background upkeep or external messages are created by these documents.

## Verification of the knowledge base itself

Check that all five specialist reports are present, relative links resolve, dated claims have citations, unresolved items remain explicit, and the root `AGENTS.md` points to the reading sequence. Documentation changes do not require unrelated app tests, deployments, or generated business-plan PDFs.
